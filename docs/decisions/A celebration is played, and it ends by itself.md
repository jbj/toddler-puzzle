# A celebration is played, and it ends by itself

A two-year-old will touch a celebration rather than watch it as a cutscene.
Every celebration therefore answers a finger immediately; there is no passive
frame a child must sit through.

**A celebration ends its arrivals, not the child's turn.** New things may stop
appearing, but whatever remains on screen stays playable until the child chooses
to leave. Nothing in a celebration advances the level by itself.

This avoids both traps: a child who clears the screen is not left with a dead
moment while arrivals are active, and a child mid-play never has the board taken
away by a clock. The finale keeps arriving indefinitely so the end of the game
is a place to stay, with the ordinary onward control available to restart.

**A celebration is not a puzzle kind.** It has no dealt pieces, targets,
difficulty, or level-table row. It owns a separate interaction layer and keeps
its progress outside the replaceable board so a viewport change cannot erase
what the child has played with.

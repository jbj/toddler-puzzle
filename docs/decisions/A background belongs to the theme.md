# A background belongs to the theme

A theme is not only a restriction on which animals may be dealt. The world
behind those animals must use the same visual language or the board reads as
two unrelated ideas.

**A theme resolves to a generated backdrop, not a fixed scene.** Authored
picture scenes are intentionally busy across the whole frame because they are
cut into pieces. A puzzle backdrop needs the opposite: environmental interest
near the horizon and quiet room where targets stand. The backdrop therefore
borrows palette and broad motifs while composing them for the current layout.

**No animal appears in a backdrop.** A painted animal beside an animal-shaped
hole looks like the answer is already on the board. Animals inside an authored
picture are different: there they are part of the picture being assembled, not
a rival to a loose piece.

**Interaction furniture stays consistent across themes.** The tray, controls,
indicators, and hole treatment are things a child learns once. A theme changes
the world, not the game language.

Every declared theme must resolve to a backdrop rather than silently falling
back to another world. Backgrounds are art, so a change is reviewed by rendering
representative boards and looking at them.

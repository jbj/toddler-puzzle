# Check silhouettes for distinctness at a glance

A two-year-old matches outline before interior detail. Two animals whose
silhouettes read alike are effectively the same piece even when their colors
and markings differ.

Visual review remains necessary but is not a complete guard for a random deal.
The art check therefore reduces silhouettes to a coarse play-scale image and
compares every pair that may be dealt together. It measures them in their shared
authored box without re-centering or normalizing: size and pose are differences
the child actually sees, and normalization would compare shapes the game never
shows.

The threshold is calibrated against accepted artwork and lives with the check.
When a pair fails, change the visible mass, pose, proportion, or eligible theme.
Raising the threshold merely permits a pair closer than the cast already
accepts; it does not make the pieces easier to distinguish.

The measure deliberately cannot decide whether an animal is drawn well. It
only protects the lower bound: outlines that can appear together must remain
different at a glance.
